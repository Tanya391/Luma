# DECISIONS.md

## 1. Why this product direction and design over the obvious alternative?

I chose to design the home page for **Luma**, an AI-powered furniture discovery experience that helps people find furniture that fits their space and personal style.

The obvious approach would have been to build a conventional furniture-commerce landing page: a large product photo, a “Shop Now” button, product categories, and promotional messaging. I rejected that because it does not communicate why an AI-powered product is meaningfully different from a normal furniture store.

Instead, I made the **user's space the starting point**. The core message is that Luma helps users go from “I like this style” to “this works in my room.” The hero therefore focuses on the outcome rather than listing technical features, while the product section demonstrates the experience with a realistic interface rather than making unsupported claims.

I also deliberately avoided fabricated testimonials, customer counts, partner logos, and performance statistics. Since Luma is a concept product for this assignment, I felt inventing social proof would make the page look more polished but less trustworthy.

## 2. One trade-off I made under the time limit

I prioritized **one polished responsive landing-page experience over building a larger number of sections or interactions**.

I could have added more product categories, complex animations, additional pages, authentication, or a fully functional shopping flow. Instead, I focused the available time on typography, spacing, responsive behavior, the hero composition, and one meaningful product interaction.

With a real week, I would validate the design with users and then expand the product experience based on that feedback. I would also connect the recommendation experience to a real product catalog and test whether the hero's value proposition actually improves signup or engagement rather than assuming it does.

## 3. Where did you use AI tools, and what did you personally verify or change afterward?

I used AI tools primarily for **ideation, implementation assistance, and debugging**. I used them to explore layout approaches, generate initial implementation ideas, and help identify responsive CSS or interaction issues.

I did not treat the generated output as final. I personally reviewed the implementation, changed the visual hierarchy and spacing, checked the page at the required desktop and mobile widths, and removed anything that felt unnecessarily decorative or unsupported.

I also manually verified the important claims and made sure the page did not rely on fabricated testimonials, fake statistics, or invented brand partnerships. The final design decisions were based on the assignment requirements and the product story I wanted the page to communicate, rather than simply accepting AI-generated suggestions.
